## Why the data is synthetic

Kingdom Hearts IV is unreleased, so no media spend, pricing, promotion or sales data exists for it. Inventing such figures and presenting them as observed would be dishonest. Simulating them from an explicit process makes the methods demonstrable and, more usefully, checkable: because the true parameters are known, the model can be scored on whether it recovers them.

## Model structure

```
simulated_sales_t = baseline + trend
                    + sum_c beta_c * hill(adstock(spend_c))
                    + price_coefficient * centred_price_t
                    + promotion_coefficient * promotion_t
                    + event_coefficient * event_t
                    + seasonality + noise
```

Each channel's spend passes through geometric adstock (carryover) and Hill saturation (diminishing returns), with decay, curve shape and half-saturation point all estimated rather than fixed. Media coefficients are constrained non-negative.

## Identifiability limitations

- Channel contributions are only partially identified. Channels whose spend moves together, or whose adstock and saturation shapes are similar, trade off against one another: the model can fit the data well while assigning credit between them differently from the truth.
- Credible intervals on channel contribution are wide (widest: video_spend, 31,185 to 102,620). Point estimates of contribution should never be quoted without them.
- Total media contribution is under-attributed: 158,103 estimated against 236,171 true. Weekly spend is fairly stable, so each channel's saturated response is close to constant, and a constant is not separable from the baseline. Only the *variation* in media is identified from observational data like this; its average level is absorbed by the intercept. Widening the baseline prior does not recover it, which is what distinguishes a structural identifiability limit from a prior artefact. Separating the level needs spend variation the data does not contain, or an experiment such as a geo holdout.
- Recovering a parameter from simulated data shows the estimator works on this process. It is not evidence of causal media effectiveness, which observational marketing data cannot establish on its own.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows that adstock, saturation, price, promotion and event effects can be estimated jointly with uncertainty, and that the estimator recovers a known process.
- It does **not** show causal media effectiveness. A fitted coefficient is an association within an assumed model; establishing causal effect needs experimentation such as geo tests or holdouts.
